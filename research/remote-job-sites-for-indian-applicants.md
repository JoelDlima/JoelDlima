# Remote Job Sites, Rated From India

**Question asked:** as an Indian applying for software roles or freelancing, how good are these five sites?

1. SkipTheDrive — https://www.skipthedrive.com/
2. Underdog.io — https://underdog.io/
3. Built In (Remote) — https://builtin.com/jobs/remote
4. Jobgether — https://jobgether.com/
5. Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent) — https://wellfound.com/jobs

Researched August 2026. Sources are linked at the bottom.

---

## Two things to settle before the rankings

**1. None of these five are freelancing platforms.** All five are full-time
employment job boards. Not one of them handles contracts, escrow, milestones,
or paying a freelancer. If freelancing is the goal, this list is the wrong list
entirely — see the freelancing section near the end.

**2. The job board is rarely the bottleneck.** For an applicant sitting in
India, what actually decides the outcome is whether the hiring company can
legally pay a person in India at all. A US company cannot directly employ
someone in India without an Indian entity; it has to run the hire through an
Employer of Record (Deel, Remote.com, Rippling — roughly $400–700/month per
employee on top of salary, with FX markups and India surcharges pushing real
cost 30–60% above headline), or engage the person as an independent
contractor. Most small US startups will not take on that overhead for a
first-time hire. Layer timezone overlap on top and the funnel narrows further.

Blind and Reddit threads say this bluntly and consistently: "worldwide remote"
for US/Canada roles is uncommon to begin with, most companies that do allow it
prioritise developers in their own timezone, and you end up competing against
the entire planet for the ~1% of listings that pay US compensation globally.
US startups that do hire in India commonly pay around half of US-equivalent
salary.

So: treat these boards as *lead sources*, and filter aggressively for
"hires globally" / "India" / "EOR available", not just "Remote".

## Rankings for an India-based applicant

Scored on usefulness **from India specifically** — not on how good the product
is in the abstract. A site can be well built and still be useless from Goa.

| # | Site | Score (India) | One-line verdict |
|---|------|---------------|------------------|
| 1 | Jobgether | 7.5 / 10 | Most genuinely global of the five. Use free, don't pay. |
| 2 | Wellfound | 7 / 10 | Two different products: great for India-based startup jobs, weak for US-remote. |
| 3 | Built In | 6 / 10 | Newly India-relevant since late 2024, but the Remote tab is still US-shaped. |
| 4 | SkipTheDrive | 4 / 10 | Free, harmless, thin. A five-minute scan, not a channel. |
| 5 | Underdog.io | 2 / 10 | Their own FAQ disqualifies people living outside the US. |

---

### 1. Jobgether — 7.5 / 10

**What it is.** A remote-first aggregator with an AI matching layer on top,
claiming 200,000+ remote listings. Belgian company, founded 2020, raised a
€1.4M seed backed by finance&invest.brussels and 4Ventures, covered by Forbes,
Tech.eu and Les Echos. It is a real, funded business — not a scam.

**Why it ranks first for India.** It is the only one of the five built
global-first rather than US-first. It maintains country-level remote job pages
(including India), and the worldwide/global-remote filters are a primary
feature rather than an afterthought. That matters more than any other single
factor on this list.

**Ratings.** Trustpilot around 4.0 / 5 across roughly 775 reviews — a
reasonable sample size, unlike most of this list.

**The honest downsides.**
- It is an **aggregator**. Duplicated listings, stale postings, and roles that
  route you to a third-party ATS anyway. Volume is not opportunity.
- **Heavy premium upsell.** Premium runs a bit under $1/day and bundles
  unlimited CV reviews, LinkedIn optimiser, match explanations, 1-click
  auto-apply and hiring manager contacts, with a 14-day refund window. A
  recurring Trustpilot complaint calls the whole thing a bait-and-switch whose
  real business is selling monthly AI resume evaluations.
- **Match quality complaints.** At least one reviewer paid for Premium and
  received matches from the Philippines. The AI match score is a
  recommendation, not a signal about your odds.
- A minority of reviewers accuse it of posting copied or fake ads to harvest
  data. The company denies selling user data. Treat the accusation as unproven
  but keep your data minimal.

**How to use it.** Free tier only. Browse, filter to global-remote, take the
company name, then apply on the company's own careers page. Auto-apply is
exactly the frictionless-volume behaviour that gets applications ignored. Do
not pay for AI resume review — no premium tier anywhere guarantees an employer
responds, and Jobgether says so itself.

### 2. Wellfound — 7 / 10

**What it is.** The old AngelList Talent. Startup-focused, salary and equity
ranges shown upfront, one-click apply.

**Why the score is split.** Wellfound is effectively two products for you:

- **India-located startup jobs** (`wellfound.com/location/india`) — genuinely
  useful. Real Indian startups hiring in India, no visa or EOR problem,
  transparent comp. This is where the score comes from.
- **US-remote startup jobs** — much weaker. The platform does let companies
  declare "hires globally" and state timezone overlap per listing, and it
  markets that capability. But the pool of startups that will actually onboard
  an India-based hire is small, and you are competing globally for it.

**Ratings.** Trustpilot 4.1 / 5 from 141 reviews. Consistently recommended on
Reddit and in job-board roundups as the best board for startup roles
specifically.

**Documented problems, several of which hit Indians harder.**
- **Applications expire unviewed.** A 2026 analysis found most Wellfound
  applications are never opened, with roughly 5–6 of every 100 applicants
  reaching interview. One-click apply floods two-person recruiting teams.
- **Ghost jobs.** Industry-wide, 18–22% of postings in a given quarter are
  positions with no intent to hire — a platform-wide problem Wellfound shares.
  Filled roles are frequently left up.
- **VPN use triggers account locks.** Wellfound's fraud-prevention system
  restricts accounts, and VPN traffic sets it off. If you routinely browse
  through a VPN, turn it off for Wellfound. Account bans with no explanation
  are one of the loudest complaints on Trustpilot.
- Reviewers have alleged US-citizen preference on the platform. Unverifiable,
  but the perception is common enough to note.
- There is a cap on simultaneous active applications, deliberately — Wellfound
  wants selective applying. Which is fine, because spraying doesn't work here
  anyway.

**How to use it.** Fill the profile out properly and set remote preferences
including timezone and whether you'll work US hours. Apply to few roles with
real customisation. Use the India location pages as a first-class channel, not
a fallback.

### 3. Built In — 6 / 10

**What it is.** A tech job board built around city-level communities, with
strong company profile pages — culture, tech stack, funding stage.

**The India-relevant development.** On 19 November 2024 Built In launched
dedicated sites across seven Indian tech hubs: **Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi,
Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai and Pune**. The company reported that postings for
international hires rose to about 25% of total inventory and international
traffic grew 40% year over year. The stated purpose is helping US employers
recruit Indian tech talent — which is precisely the pipeline that matters here.

**Ratings.** G2 4.3 / 5 (against LinkedIn's 4.5), praised for company insight
depth and responsive support; criticised for a thin senior-level selection.

**The catch.** The `/jobs/remote` page in the screenshot — the actual link
being evaluated — is still overwhelmingly US-tagged. Browsing it returns page
after page of US state locations. The India value is in the **city sites**, not
the Remote tab.

**How to use it.** Go to the Bengaluru / Hyderabad / Pune / Mumbai pages
instead of the remote feed. Use the company profiles as research even when you
apply elsewhere — the tech stack and stage detail is better than most boards
and it's good interview prep material.

### 4. SkipTheDrive — 4 / 10

**What it is.** A remote job aggregator running since 2013, owned and operated
by one person (Pete Metz). Roughly 28,000 jobs from 2,800+ companies. No
registration, no fee, no paywall, no dark patterns.

**Positives.** Genuinely free and genuinely legitimate — the listings it
surfaces are real listings. Clean interface, unobtrusive ads, job alerts
without an account. Trustpilot sentiment is mildly positive; one reviewer
called it "a diamond in the rough."

**Why it still scores low from India.**
- **Composition.** Customer service roles make up a large share of listings.
  Software is present but not the centre of gravity.
- **De facto US-focused.** The site markets itself as global and mentions
  digital nomads, but makes no explicit international commitment, and in
  practice the inventory reads as US telecommute work.
- **Aggregated and lightly vetted.** By its own reviews: outdated postings, and
  some listings that aren't actually remote. Not every listing is checked.
- **No moat.** Anything here is usually visible on larger boards too.

**How to use it.** A weekly five-minute scan with the software filters on.
Costs nothing, occasionally surfaces something. Never your primary channel.

### 5. Underdog.io — 2 / 10

**Skip this one.** Not because it's bad — because it explicitly excludes you.

Underdog.io's own FAQ states: *"If you live outside of the U.S. and/or hold a
non-transferable visa (e.g. B1, L1, J1), we are not likely to feature you in an
Underdog.io candidate cohort."* The model is a curated weekly batch — roughly
the top 10% of applicants — sent to founders and recruiters at mostly NYC and
SF startups. If you aren't in the batch, nothing happens. Some partner
companies do sponsor visas, but only for candidates **already living in the US
with an existing transferable visa**.

Two further filters worth knowing: they state candidates do best when seeking
full-time roles, **not recently graduated from a coding bootcamp**, and not
requiring sponsorship.

**Ratings are misleading here.** G2 shows 4.7 / 5 — from **12 reviews**, and
those reviewers are *employers* praising candidate quality and the small
curated pool. That number says nothing about a job seeker's experience, let
alone an Indian one.

**Status.** Alive and operating. Acquired by Quantum Leap Innovations, closing
2 January 2025, with Travis Lindemoen as president.

**Verdict.** Revisit only if you relocate to the US on a transferable visa.
Until then it is a closed door with a nice paint job.

## A caveat about every rating on this page

G2, Capterra and Slashdot scores for recruiting platforms are dominated by
**employer** reviews, because employers are the paying customers. A 4.7 on G2
means recruiters like the hiring funnel. It tells you nothing about whether
your application gets read. Trustpilot skews the other way — job seekers, and
disproportionately angry ones. Read both, trust neither at face value.

Reddit itself could not be fetched directly during this research (reddit.com
blocks the crawler used). The Reddit-derived sentiment above comes from
secondary summaries of r/jobs, r/cscareerquestions and r/developersIndia
threads, plus TeamBlind threads that were directly reachable. The Blind
material on India-to-US remote hiring was the most consistent and the most
pessimistic.

## The freelancing answer (since none of the five do this)

If freelancing is the actual goal, the relevant platforms are:

| Platform | Fee | Notes for India |
|---|---|---|
| **Upwork** | 0–15% + Connects to bid | Highest volume, widest selection, best for long-term client relationships. The default starting point. |
| **Contra** | **0% commission** | Zero commission including on hourly. Best economics if you can bring or find your own clients. |
| **Toptal** | Client-side | $60–200+/hr, Fortune 500 clients, but sub-3% acceptance. Realistic only with several years of strong experience. |
| **Fiverr** | Flat 20% | Productised gigs rather than client relationships. |
| **Arc.dev** | — | Aggregates remote dev jobs; "Verified Developer" vetting lets you skip early interview rounds. |
| **Braintrust / Gun.io** | Low/none | Vetted networks, senior-leaning. |

**Getting paid.** Payoneer is the most widely used route into an Indian bank
account and works with Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer and most global platforms;
Wise is the common alternative. Keep every FIRC/FIRA — you will need proof of
foreign inward remittance.

**Tax, briefly and non-authoritatively.** Services sold from India to a
recipient abroad, paid in foreign currency, qualify as **export of services**
and are **zero-rated** — 0% GST charged. GST registration becomes mandatory
above ₹20 lakh of total service income in a financial year (₹10 lakh in special
category states); Indian and foreign income both count toward that threshold.
File a **Letter of Undertaking (Form GST RFD-11)** to invoice in USD/EUR/GBP
without paying 18% IGST upfront — it's valid one financial year (April–March)
and must be refiled before each new year. Many exporters register voluntarily
below the threshold just to file the LUT and claim input tax credit. Confirm
all of this with a CA before acting on it.

## Practical playbook

1. **Filter for the constraint, not the keyword.** Search "hires globally",
   "India", "EOR", "contractor", "async" — not "Remote". A "Remote" tag on a US
   board usually means remote *within the US*.
2. **Prefer companies already hiring in India.** If a company has one India
   contractor, the legal and payroll path exists. If it has none, you are
   asking them to build one for you.
3. **Do not spray.** One-click apply is precisely why applications go unread.
   Few applications, each customised, beats a hundred auto-applies —
   measurably, on these platforms.
4. **Never pay for AI resume review.** No platform guarantees an employer
   responds; several say so in their own terms.
5. **Turn the VPN off** on Wellfound and similar platforms. It trips fraud
   detection and gets accounts locked.
6. **Freelancing is the faster first door than full-time US remote.** A
   contractor engagement doesn't require an entity, an EOR, or a payroll
   decision — which is exactly why it converts more easily from India.
7. **Expect the compensation discount.** US startups hiring in India commonly
   pay roughly half of US-equivalent. Worth knowing before negotiating, and
   still often well above local market.
