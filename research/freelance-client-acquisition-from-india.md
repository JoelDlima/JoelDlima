# Getting Paid in Dollars to Build Websites, From India, As a Student

**The actual goal:** final-year engineering student in Goa. Build websites for
new founders / CEOs / small teams. Side gigs and short projects, not full-time
employment. Network, get connected, and earn in USD/EUR so the money is worth
several times what the same hours pay locally.

Companion to [remote-job-sites-for-indian-applicants.md](./remote-job-sites-for-indian-applicants.md),
which rated five job boards. This document answers the real question, because
**four of those five boards are the wrong tool for this** — and one of them
becomes useful only if you flip how you use it.

Researched August 2026.

---

## The reframe

Those five sites answer "who is hiring an employee?" You need "who just started
a company and has an ugly website?" Different question, different infrastructure.

Concretely, at ₹95.5 to the dollar (mid-August 2026):

| You charge | You receive | Context |
|---|---|---|
| $500 | ~₹47,750 | One landing page, ~a week of evening work |
| $1,500 | ~₹1.43 lakh | A good landing page with custom design |
| $3,000 | ~₹2.87 lakh | A small multi-page marketing site |

Three $1,500 projects across a semester is roughly ₹4.3 lakh — a serious
fraction of a fresher SDE annual salary, earned in evenings, while still a
student. That is the arbitrage, and it is real. But it only works if you price
per project. The moment you quote hourly you get benchmarked against the going
rate for "beginner freelance developer," which the market prices at **$8–12/hr**.
That number is the trap. Avoid it entirely.

---

## The one thing that decides everything: positioning

Every source on client acquisition converges on the same point, and it is the
highest-leverage thing in this document.

**Do not present yourself as "web developer."** That competes with every
freelancer on earth on price, which is a competition you lose from India by
design.

Present yourself as a narrow specialist. The pattern that works looks like:

> "Framer + Next.js landing pages for pre-seed SaaS founders. Shipped in 7 days."

Narrow beats broad. "Brand Identity Package" outsells "Graphic Design"; the same
logic holds for development. A founder who needs exactly that thing will pay a
premium for someone who does only that thing, and won't think to compare your
rate to a generalist's.

Pick one stack (Framer, or Webflow, or Next.js) and one customer (pre-seed
founders, or indie hackers, or agencies needing overflow). You can widen later.

## Tier 1: build the credentials that generate inbound

These three moves compound. Do them in this order.

### 1. Framer Expert directory — the single best first move

The requirements are startlingly low:

- **One** completed client project in Framer
- Fully functional and responsive
- Original work, not a template edit — show real layout, animation, or
  interaction thinking

That's it. Approval gets you a profile in the public Framer Experts directory,
a Framer Expert badge **on your Contra profile**, and visibility to clients
actively searching for Framer people.

One client project is a bar you can clear this month. Do a free or cheap first
project specifically to clear it, then apply. Inbound from a directory is worth
vastly more than any amount of cold outreach, and almost nobody at your stage
realises the bar is this low.

### 2. Webflow Certified Partner — the follow-up

Higher bar: an active Freelancer or Agency Workspace plan, a pre-qualification
assessment, **three** new client sites built as a Foundations member, and a
portfolio review. Rewards are bigger — directory listing, a **client-matching
service**, higher commission rates, beta access, event invites.

This is a three-to-six-month goal, not a this-week goal. Framer first.

### 3. Contra — your billing layer and portfolio home

- **0% commission.** You pay only Stripe's processing fee (2.9% + $0.30).
- Profiles function like personal websites — good for a developer.
- Portfolio-driven: load **5+ case studies with visuals**, not a résumé.
- Your Framer Expert badge displays here.

Important nuance from the reviews: Contra's *native* job volume is thin
compared to Upwork. Its real value is as **zero-fee payment and client
management infrastructure for clients you source yourself**. That's exactly your
situation. Every founder you meet on X or Indie Hackers gets routed through
Contra to contract and pay you, and you keep 100%.

## Tier 2: where the founders actually are

You want people who *just* started something. Ranked by signal for your profile:

**Product Hunt.** Daily feed of freshly launched products. A founder launching
this week has a product and, very often, a landing page that undermines it.
Comment usefully on the launch first. The window of relevance is days, so check
regularly.

**Indie Hackers (~140K) and r/indiehackers (~100K).** Where bootstrapped solo
founders actually congregate, share revenue milestones, and ask for feedback.
These people have money, no design team, and no patience for agencies. Answer
questions for weeks before you ever mention that you take work — pitching cold
into these communities gets you ignored or banned.

**X / #buildinpublic.** Consistently named the highest-value channel for
indie-hacker reach. Reply substantively to founders shipping things. Post your
own builds. This is slow — months — but it's how you get *inbound* rather than
begging, and it's the closest thing to the "networking and connecting" you
described.

**Recently-funded startup lists.** Funded means budget; just-funded means they
need a site now. [VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/directory/funding/seed) has
33,000+ seed companies with funding amounts and founding teams;
[Growth List](https://growthlist.co/seed-startups/) has 25,000+ with
decision-maker contacts, updated weekly;
[Fundraise Insider](https://fundraiseinsider.com/blog/seed-startups/) ships
250+ new funded-company contacts weekly. The
[YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies) is free and comprehensive.

**Upwork — as a bootstrap only.** Use it to get your first two or three
five-star reviews and nothing more. 0–15% fees plus Connects to bid, but it has
volume you don't have yet. Once you have proof, source directly and bill through
Contra. The standard advice for intermediate freelancers is exactly this split:
Upwork for discovery, Contra for the money.

## Inverting the five sites from the original question

Two of them are genuinely useful here — just not for applying.

**Wellfound and Built In are lead lists.** Wellfound shows funding stage and
comp for startups; Built In has deep company profiles with tech stack, funding
stage, and culture. A startup posting its first engineering roles is a startup
with fresh money and an in-progress web presence. Read the listings as a
**directory of founders to pitch**, not jobs to apply for. Built In added
dedicated Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai and Pune sites
in November 2024, so there's Indian-company coverage too — though for your
purchasing-power goal you want the US/EU companies.

**Jobgether and SkipTheDrive** — nothing for you. Aggregated FTE listings.

**Underdog.io** — actively excludes you. Its FAQ states people living outside
the US are unlikely to be featured in a candidate cohort. Ignore it completely.

## Cold outreach that isn't spam

The research is unanimous that templates fail and precision works. The shape
that gets replies:

1. **Find a specific defect.** Their site loads in 6s on mobile. Their hero
   doesn't say what the product does. No CTA above the fold. Broken on a phone.
2. **Lead with the observation, not with yourself.** One or two sentences.
3. **Give something free and small first.** A short audit. A Lighthouse score. A
   quick redesigned hero as a screenshot. Do the work before asking.
4. **Make a tiny ask.** Not "can I build your website" — "want me to send the
   fixed version?"

Ten of these a day, genuinely researched, beats two hundred templated emails.
The free-audit-into-paid-project path is the most commonly cited working motion
for web development clients.

**Being a student is an asset here — don't hide it.** Early founders like
hungry, fast, affordable, and personally invested. Lead with the work, mention
the student thing as context. Your GitHub (Road_SOS, CropGuardv2) and portfolio
are the proof; keep the portfolio sharp because every prospect will open it.

## Pricing ladder

| Stage | Price per landing page | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First 2 projects | $300–500 | Buying testimonials and case studies, not income |
| After 3 case studies | $800–1,500 | Market rate for custom design + basic CRO |
| Multi-page marketing site | $2,000–3,500 | Once you have a directory listing |
| Maintenance retainer | $200–400/month | The compounding play — do this |

Market context: freelancers charge **$300–1,500+** per landing page; the
$500–1,500 band buys custom design, basic conversion work, and limited copy.
Copywriting adds $300–1,500 on top; each integration $50–300. Charge for those
separately — they're the easiest upsells you'll ever make.

Rules:
- **Never quote hourly.** Quote the project.
- **Never mention your location as a discount.** You're not cheap, you're
  focused.
- **50% upfront**, always. Written scope. Two revision rounds included, more
  billed.
- **Don't transfer the repo or domain until the final payment clears.**
- Retainers are the difference between a side gig and an income. Every finished
  site should end with a maintenance offer.

## Getting the money to India

The 2026 picture for Indian freelancers:

**Wise — your default.** Near mid-market rates, conversion from ~0.47%, 1–2
business days to an Indian bank account. On $30,000 a year, total fees run
roughly $150–200. Use this whenever a client can send to account details.

**Payoneer — only for marketplaces.** Its strength is 2,000+ integrations
(Upwork, Fiverr, and most platforms), so you'll need it if you use Upwork. But
its exchange rate sits 2–3% below mid-market, costing $800–1,000+ on the same
$30,000, and **the Payoneer prepaid Mastercard isn't available in India**.

**Stripe — avoid in India.** Invite-only, cards only, effective cost around 6.3%
once the 4.3% card fee and 2% conversion stack.

**PayPal — last resort.** Only if a client insists.

**Keep every FIRC/FIRA.** Proof of foreign inward remittance matters at tax time
and if a bank ever asks.

### Tax, briefly — confirm with a CA

- Services sold from India to a client abroad, paid in foreign currency, are
  **export of services** and **zero-rated**: 0% GST charged.
- GST registration is mandatory above **₹20 lakh** total service income in a
  financial year (₹10 lakh in special-category states). Indian and foreign
  income both count. As a student doing side projects you're likely well under
  this — but track it.
- If you do register, file a **Letter of Undertaking (Form GST RFD-11)** to
  invoice in USD/EUR without paying 18% IGST upfront. Valid one financial year
  (April–March); refile before each new one.
- Freelance income is taxable regardless of GST status. Keep invoices from day
  one.

## A realistic plan given your constraints

You have a Visteon internship (Jul–Oct 2026) and final-year coursework. Assume
10–15 hours a week, not 40. Plan accordingly:

**Weeks 1–3.** Pick a lane — Framer is the recommendation, because the expert
bar is one project and the directory is real inbound. Build two spec landing
pages for real companies you like, as portfolio pieces. Write them up as case
studies: problem, what you changed, why.

**Weeks 3–6.** Land one paying client at $300–500 via Upwork or a warm contact.
Deliver it exceptionally. Get a written testimonial and permission to show the
work.

**Week 6.** Apply to the Framer Experts directory with that project. Build the
Contra profile with 5+ case studies in parallel.

**Ongoing, in the background from day one.** Post on X about what you're
building. Answer questions on Indie Hackers. Comment on Product Hunt launches.
Send ten researched cold emails a week to recently-funded seed startups. This
channel takes months to warm up, which is exactly why it starts now.

**Months 3–6.** Raise to $800–1,500. Convert one client to a retainer. Start the
three Webflow client sites if you want that directory too.

**Do one project at a time.** A missed deadline in your first year costs more
than the second project earns — your entire asset at this stage is the
testimonial.
